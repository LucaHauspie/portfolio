## Intro

Gabber Unleashed is a website that invites gabbers to leave the rat race for a life off the grid, and keep their love for hardcore while they’re at it.

## Body

The brief paired a target audience with living off the grid. I chose gabbers, who already escape the daily grind every weekend at 200 BPM. Their biggest question is whether they can still rave out there. The site answers it with tongue-in-cheek tips (a moshpit with goats, beats from chickens on bubble wrap, a rave cave) and an invitation to an off-grid festival. I drew every illustration in a hand-cut, angular style, then designed and coded the site myself.

## Question

How might we make the rave, the music and the community work in an off-the-grid life?

## Step 1: Research

I dug into gabber culture: the sound (a relentless kick drum, hi-hats, eerie voice samples), the look (bald heads, tracksuits, bomber jackets, Air Max) and the weekly cycle of work, rave and recovery. Documentaries and an interview made the core clear. The rave is an escape from society, and the community matters more than anything. I also listed what off-grid life takes away and collected eleven websites to find interesting content that I could integrate.

## Step 2: Concept

Association brainstorms and crazy 8s turned those values into tips: Drop It (beats from chickens on bubble wrap and stones on hollow logs), The Bleat Pit (a moshpit with goats), a rave cave with a handmade disco ball, the condor as the ultimate gabber bird, and hakken with a hoe on your heel. The pitch was approved with one warning: don’t mock the audience.

## Step 3: Style

Classic 90s gabber visuals are lo-fi and heavy on effects, but the new generation is brutalist with heavy type. A tip to mix that with Saul Bass gave me the direction: angular and simple, like something made by hand, which suits a life off the grid. It was my first time illustrating. The title got more angular, and the mask logo came out of a single sketch.

## Step 4: Concept image

My first hero was rejected because it didn’t show the concept (and the goat looked like a dog). A teacher suggested chains, which clicked with “unleashed”. The new image has rats chained to an office building on one side and a gabber walking free towards the forest on the other. Chains became the thread through the whole site.

## Step 5: Feedback rounds

Second-years, third-years and teachers kept pointing at the same things: the left-right ping-pong layout, uneven stroke widths, text that was too big, harsh black-and-white contrast and loose alignment. I gave every illustration an orange detail, put one tip’s text inside its illustration and gave that section an orange background.

## Step 6: Final

The Join Us page mixes content formats from my research: expectations at a glance, reviews and a sign-up form. I reused earlier sketches as references, made font sizes and line heights consistent, and kept the big custom titles while setting article titles in a real font for accessibility. Then I coded the whole site in HTML and CSS.
