## Intro

MYST, found in Antwerp. A brief by Visit Antwerp: get young travellers into the city’s fashion scene without it feeling like advertising.

## Body

Six weeks, four Devine students and two CMD students from Rotterdam. I did the visual design and helped build the installation.

## Concept 1: The problem

Young Urban Travellers (18 to 36) know Antwerp but rarely plan a visit. They hate planning and love side quests. So MYST hides the city’s most personal stores in mist.

## Concept 2: The MYST box

Antwerp is known worldwide for its fashion scene, from the Antwerp Six to its independent stores, which is why we built the concept around it. The box is an installation in the city: pick one of nine Antwerp styles on the iPad and the outfit is projected onto a mannequin, with its story, its store and a QR code to the app.

## Concept 3: The app

Antwerp covered in fog that clears as you explore. Pins reveal stores, boutiques, fashion history and workshops, and you share them with your crew.

## Question

How can we build a digital experience that subtly entices young people to discover Antwerp fashion, without it feeling like advertising?

## Step 1: Research & concept

Interviews with Gen Z in Rotterdam showed that people know Antwerp but rarely plan a trip there. The key insight: they don’t enjoy planning, they love side quests. That led to our HMW question, four design principles and a first pitch, a map covered in mist.

## Step 2: Refining & validating

For the visual identity I started from Antwerp itself. The city’s pixel-grid campaigns and the fashion festival’s serif grew into MYST’s pixelated serif logo, and my styleboard added blur, grain and red accents. We validated the concept with a survey, three personas and a user journey.

## Step 3: Testing & prototyping

Projector tests showed the box had to be closed on three sides with a plexiglass front. A small test site proved MadMapper could switch outfits from code. Meanwhile we researched nine Antwerp fashion styles and iterated on the iPad screens.

## Step 4: Building the box

We assembled the box, the iPad stand and the mannequin, and dressed it in white fabric for a bigger projection surface. I worked on the installation with Alexander and learned the basics of projection mapping in MadMapper: fitting each outfit onto the mannequin and switching it the moment someone taps the iPad.

## Step 5: Final design & development

Alexander and I finished every app screen, from the custom icons to the illustrations. The iPad got a loading screen and a QR code that sends visitors straight to the app, while the app was built in React Native with Supabase.

## Step 6: Expo

We presented MYST in Kortrijk to the teachers and the client: box, iPad, app and presentation, all in black, white and red.

## Biggest challenge
